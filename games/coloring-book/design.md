# Coloring Book: design sheet

Approved by the owner 2026-11-20 ("yes, go"). Not in the brief, so every row is "new". First version: sandbox canvas only; outline pictures come later.

| Row | Content |
|---|---|
| Pitch | A big empty canvas and a tray of real-feeling tools. Oil paint is wet and thick: a new stroke pushes the paint already there aside and mixes with it, leaving bristle streaks and ridges that catch the light. Watercolor is see-through and spreads on the wet paper. Crayon is waxy and grainy. A simple brush paints plain color, with a size and a softness (hard edge to fuzzy edge). Stickers are dragged on top. No goal: paint whatever you want, keep it in your gallery. |
| Fantasy | I am a painter with a real studio's paints. |
| Toy | The whole game is the toy: smearing wet oil paint around and watching colors mix and push. |
| Seconds loop | Pick a tool and a color, make a stroke, see the paint behave like that material. |
| Minutes loop | Make one picture, keep it in the gallery, start a new canvas. No failure; nothing to retry. |
| Days loop | The gallery grows; old pictures can be opened and painted on again. |
| Mechanics | Tools: oil brush, watercolor, crayon, simple brush, stickers. Each tool has its own color choice (a row of paint pots). The simple brush has size and softness controls shown as pictures (a small/big dot, a sharp/fuzzy dot), no numbers. Oil: each dab of the brush puts down its own paint and picks up some of the paint under it, so it carries and mixes color along the stroke and runs dry over a long stroke (standard technique: ArtRage/Rebelle-style paint pickup); paint has thickness lit from one side. Watercolor: see-through layers that darken where they overlap, darker rims at the edges, a little spreading while wet (standard technique: Curtis et al. 1997, computer-generated watercolor). Crayon: color only lands on the paper's raised grain. Stickers: drag from the tray, move them, they stay on top. Undo and new canvas. Added 21 Nov (owner): acrylic; 3 brush shapes per medium; 5 sizes; hold a colour for more paint (3 dots); pressure; canvases (watercolour paper, smooth paper, cotton, linen); palettes; a see-through guide picture; delete the open picture (two presses); Ctrl+Z takes back. |
| Dynamics | Discovering that colors mix on the canvas (blue into yellow makes green), that oil can be pushed around while watercolor sinks in, that softness changes the edge. |
| Kinds of fun | Aims at: sensation, expression, discovery. Not: challenge, narrative, fellowship. |
| Competence | Every mark is exactly where his finger went and looks like the material he picked. |
| Autonomy | All of it: tool, color, what to paint. |
| Relatedness | Show the gallery to parents. |
| Mastery | None required; mixing new colors on purpose is there for a player who wants it. |
| What would kill the fun | Lag on the full-screen canvas (wet paint is heavy to compute); oil mixing turning everything brown too fast; wiping a picture by accident; controls too small or too many; a second finger or palm making marks. |
| Prototype must answer | Does wet oil feel wet and stay smooth on the PC's screen? Can he pick tools and colors alone? Which tool does he come back to? What should a second finger do (nothing, or paint too)? |
