# Tower Builder: level redesign proposals (not yet approved)

Built on branch `level-proposals` (`tools/make-levels.py` there, new piece `abacus`); every suggested build passes the full test and every wrong choice fails (harness). Pictures: `proposals/NN-key.png`, outline left, suggested build right, orange = headpiece lowered in the test.

Owner's rules for these levels:
- middle and higher levels must not be a solid wall; introduce new shapes;
- keep historical validity (sources below; anything not verified is marked);
- the headpiece is an important part of that building;
- Lion Gate: the headpiece is the lion triangle; the builder also uses triangle pieces; more complex.
- Halicarnassus must differ from the Parthenon (owner's idea: wooden support beams for a roof).
- Pharos must be tall and clearly harder than the middle levels.

Ideas not built yet:
- Pharos harder: very little spare stone (exact pile), windows whose lintels must be stone, the round top tier on a narrow octagonal tier so it must be centred; stronger wind for its height.
- Halicarnassus with wood: the stepped roof built course by course on wooden posts and beams inside the hall, taken out at the end like the arch forms; needs heavier outer courses as counterweight or it falls inward.
- Lion Gate more complex: the bastion beside the approach (rectangular, 14.8 x 7.2 m), cyclopean walls; the gate narrows as it rises (3.10 m at the threshold, 2.78 m under the lintel).

Sources: Lion Gate (Wikipedia: jambs and lintel 4.5 x 2.0 x 0.8 m, relief base 3.60 m, over 3 m high, bastion); Ziggurat of Ur (Britannica: reed matting, three tiers, staircases, temple of Nanna); Ishtar Gate (worldhistory.org: door and roof of cedar and bronze); Mausoleum (Pliny via worldhistory.org and livius.org: 36 columns, 24 steps, quadriga, lions at the pyramid base); Pharos (Wikipedia: square/octagonal/round tiers, Triton statues on the corners and Poseidon or Zeus on top, from Roman coins); Karnak (University of Memphis: taller central nave, clerestory grilles).
